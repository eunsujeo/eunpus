# T5 — Stewardship Failure Modes

> Generalized — institutional WaaS architecture corpus, stewardship practice failure catalog.
> All generalized reasoning is ★ Hypothesis.
> No "good stewards don't fail" claim. No moralization. No ideology hardening.

---

## 0. Why a steward-internal failure catalog

R10 catalogues **corpus-level failure modes** — how the corpus, as an institutional artifact, fails (ontology collapse, contradiction accumulation, silent rewrite, vendor recapture, etc.).

T5 catalogues a different class: **stewardship-internal failure modes** — how the practice of stewardship itself degrades. These are not corpus failures; they are **practitioner failures** that produce corpus failures downstream.

The distinction matters because the **internal degradation** of stewardship is usually invisible from outside. A stewardship that has become rigid, captured, fatigued, or ideologically hardened may continue to produce the **form** of compliance — audit trails are filed, registries are maintained, snapshots are taken — while losing the **substance**. By the time the corpus exhibits R10 failure modes, T5 failures have typically been in motion for years.

T5 names them so that stewards can recognize them in **themselves**, not just in the corpus.

---

## 1. Core thesis

> The greatest risk to a mature reasoning system is not external criticism, but internal degradation masked as refinement.
> Stewards who cannot name their own failure modes will exhibit them.
> The catalog exists not to assign blame but to make degradation **visible enough to address**.

## 2. ≠ propositions

- Stability ≠ health

- Consensus ≠ validity

- Discipline performance ≠ discipline substance
- "We have always done it this way" ≠ this way is right

## 3. The 10 stewardship failure modes

★ Hypothesis — minimum viable catalog:

| # | Failure mode | Signal | Time horizon |
|---|--------------|--------|--------------|
| **SF1** | **Stewardship capture** | One steward or sub-coalition shapes outcomes disproportionately | Months-years |
| **SF2** | **Ideology hardening** | Steward defends positions because they are personal commitments | Years |
| **SF3** | **Hidden simplification** | Steward simplifies content "for accessibility"; load-bearing nuance lost | Months |
| **SF4** | **Authority centralization** | Decision authority migrates to one role / one person | Years |
| **SF5** | **Drift normalization** | Drift signals observed but accepted as "natural" | Quarters |
| **SF6** | **Maintenance fatigue** | Stewards over time treat discipline as burden; corners cut | Years |
| **SF7** | **Conceptual fossilization** | Stewards refuse to revisit decisions; "settled" status accumulates | Years-decades |
| **SF8** | **Institutional complacency** | Stewardship perceives itself as successful; surveillance discipline relaxes | Years |
| **SF9** | **Form-over-substance compliance** | Stewards perform R-series steps without engaging substance | Quarters-years |
| **SF10** | **Conflict avoidance** | Stewards smooth disagreements to preserve group cohesion | Continuous |

Each is described below with: **signal**, **cause**, **how it masks itself**, **mitigation practice**, **recovery**.

---

## 4. SF1 — Stewardship capture

**Signal:** A single steward or small coalition exerts disproportionate influence over outcomes. Other stewards defer; reviewer dissent is rare; cluster lead changes have minimal effect on decisions.

**Cause:** Tenure asymmetry, expertise gap, social dynamics, founder effect, institutional concentration.

**How it masks itself:** "The captured steward is just the most knowledgeable." Disagreement is framed as inexperience.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Rotating stewardship (R5 §11) with overlap, not replacement.
- Explicit reviewer dissent capture — audit trail records reviewer-by-reviewer verdicts.
- Periodic "blind review" exercises where steward identity is concealed during review.
- New-steward voice protection — incoming stewards' positions are weighted equally during their first year.

**Recovery:** Stewardship rotation accelerated; captured steward moved to advisory role rather than decision role; audit trail review for past N years to identify decisions that may warrant revisit.

---

## 5. SF2 — Ideology hardening

**Signal:** Steward consistently defends a position that has been challenged by multiple sources; defends across multiple cycles without engaging the challenge substance; uses appeals to authority or tenure.

**Cause:** Position becomes personal commitment; identity fused with intellectual stance; revisiting position feels like self-betrayal.

**How it masks itself:** "I have engaged the challenge; I just find it unconvincing." Steward conflates "I disagree" with "I have steelmanned and concluded the challenge fails."

**Mitigation practice:**
- T2 steelman discipline enforced in contradiction conversations.
- Periodic position-rotation exercise: each steward writes a brief defense of a position they personally disagree with.
- Reviewer rotation prevents personal-investment accumulation.
- "Why this matters" annotations (T3) document why an invariant is load-bearing, separately from who advocated for it.

**Recovery:** Affected positions reopened for review with rotated steward composition; ideology-hardened steward optionally redirected to less position-laden domain.

---

## 6. SF3 — Hidden simplification

**Signal:** Recent revisions consistently shorten content; nuance markers (★, conditional clauses, qualifier phrases) decrease over time; "this is too complex" feedback appears in reviewer notes.

**Cause:** Pressure for "readability" — from sponsors, executives, AI assistants, new readers. The pressure feels reasonable; the cost is invisible.

**How it masks itself:** "We made it more accessible." Lost nuance is not surfaced because it has been removed.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Pre-simplification snapshot (R7) before any simplification revision.
- Diff review specifically looking for removed qualifiers and ★ markers.
- "Cold-read accessibility test" (T3 §10) executed without simplification; accessibility is a property of structure and exemplification, not nuance removal.
- C5 (audience reading paths) used for accessibility instead of doc simplification.

**Recovery:** Simplification revisions audited; lost nuance restored from R7 snapshots; future simplifications routed through explicit nuance-preservation review.

---

## 7. SF4 — Authority centralization

**Signal:** Decision authority migrates over time toward one role (e.g., "lead steward") or one person; reviewers increasingly defer rather than independently judge; published verdicts reflect single-author voice rather than collective discussion.

**Cause:** Institutional pressure for clear accountability; deference culture; efficiency optimization; gradual abdication by other stewards.

**How it masks itself:** "We have clear ownership now." Other stewards present but functionally absent.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Quorum discipline (R5) enforced; quorum cannot be satisfied by single-author proposals.
- Mandatory reviewer dissent capture — even when reviewers agree, the audit trail records independent verdicts.
- Decision authority is **distributed by class** (R5 §3); no single role holds C0-C8 authority.
- "No single owner" invariant (R5 §4) explicitly tracked.

**Recovery:** Authority distribution explicitly re-balanced; lead-steward role power reduced; decision authority delegated back to cluster leads and steward council.

---

## 8. SF5 — Drift normalization

**Signal:** T1 drift signals observed in monthly / quarterly reports; signals discussed; no action taken; pattern accepted as background.

**Cause:** Drift response is effortful; "natural evolution" framing legitimizes inaction; signal fatigue from too many signals.

**How it masks itself:** "Drift is part of corpus evolution; we are tracking it." Tracking without response is observation, not stewardship.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Each drift signal in quarterly report has a **named status**: observe / annotate / sense-version / supersession / charter review (T1 §14). No signal is left without status.
- Open signals tracked across cycles; signals that remain at "observe" for ≥ 3 cycles are escalated.
- "Drift action ratio" tracked — fraction of drift signals that produced corpus action vs remained observational.

**Recovery:** Backlog of normalized drift signals reviewed; categorized into "still observation-worthy" vs "should have been acted on"; latter category processed through R5 governance.

---

## 9. SF6 — Maintenance fatigue

**Signal:** Stewards visibly tired; cooling-off period rushed; discipline corners cut ("just this once"); informal stewardship time decreases; T1 weekly sampling skipped.

**Cause:** Stewardship is sustained effort over years; pace and load not calibrated to human sustainability; institutional rewards not aligned with stewardship work.

**How it masks itself:** "We are very busy with operational priorities." Fatigue is rationalized as scheduling rather than recognized as discipline degradation.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Stewardship load is **explicitly budgeted** by institution; not "additional duties as assigned."
- Rotation periodicity prevents indefinite individual tenure.
- Stewardship handoff exercises (T3 §12) include load-handoff, not just knowledge-handoff.
- "Maintenance fatigue indicator" tracked: cooling-off abbreviations, skipped sampling cycles, dropped review meetings — each is a signal.

**Recovery:** Load redistribution; institutional sponsor re-engaged on stewardship budget; affected stewards offered sabbatical or rotation; backlog audit for missed disciplines.

---

## 10. SF7 — Conceptual fossilization

**Signal:** "Settled" status accumulates on positions, ontology choices, cluster structure; revisit proposals rejected without engagement; new stewards told "we settled that years ago."

**Cause:** Decision exhaustion; preference for stability; protective stance toward prior stewards' decisions; conflation of "stable invariant" with "frozen invariant."

**How it masks itself:** "The corpus has matured." Maturity becomes immobility.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Stable invariants (R6 Class A) reviewed on **decade cadence**, not "never revisited."
- New stewards explicitly invited to challenge settled positions during first year.
- R3 reopens resolved contradictions when new evidence arises (R3 §10).
- Charter review (R5 C8) periodically asks: "what have we treated as settled that should be revisited?"

**Recovery:** Settled-positions audit; positions surveyed for evidence of recent challenge engagement; positions with zero engagement in N years reopened for re-affirmation review.

---

## 11. SF8 — Institutional complacency

**Signal:** Stewardship perceives itself as successful; surveillance disciplines (T1 sampling, R7 reconstruction exercises, T3 succession map) relaxed; "we have solved this" framing.

**Cause:** Initial success producing confidence; lack of recent failures; comparison to weaker corpora generating relative complacency.

**How it masks itself:** "We have a mature stewardship." Success becomes premise rather than continuous practice.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Honest-failure exercise (R10 §18) — stewards write annually about most likely failure modes.
- External review periodically — non-stewards (academic, industry, regulatory) read corpus and provide critique.
- Forking encouraged (R5 §12); divergent forks reveal complacency in original.
- Steward humility commitment in T0 stewardship spirit (10 commitments).

**Recovery:** Surveillance disciplines reactivated at full cadence; external review commissioned; honest-failure exercise published.

---

## 12. SF9 — Form-over-substance compliance

**Signal:** R-series steps executed (R5 cooling-off observed, R7 snapshots taken, R3 registry entries created); but the **substance** is shallow (reviewer engagement during cooling-off perfunctory; snapshots taken but unread; registry entries without conversation depth).

**Cause:** Compliance metrics reward form; substance is harder to measure; institutional pressure for "audit-ready" stewardship.

**How it masks itself:** "We are R-series compliant." Form is observable; substance is not.

**Mitigation practice:**
- Audit trail review specifically for substance — sample N entries quarterly; check reviewer rationale depth.
- Random reconstruction exercises (T3 §10) — test whether snapshots are actually reconstructable.
- Reader-facing audit — readers occasionally asked: do the snapshot annotations help you understand prior positions? If no, snapshots are form-only.
- "Substance over form" stewardship commitment explicitly named.

**Recovery:** Substance audit identifies form-only artifacts; affected artifacts enriched with substance; reviewer engagement protocols deepened.

---

## 13. SF10 — Conflict avoidance

**Signal:** Steward conversations reach quick consensus; T3 contradictions rare; reviewer dissent rare; disagreement framed as "alignment problem" to resolve through discussion rather than as substance to preserve.

**Cause:** Group cohesion preference; institutional culture; new-steward social pressure; sponsor preference for "unified team voice."

**How it masks itself:** "We have great collaboration." Collaboration becomes false coherence.

**Mitigation practice:**
- T2 contradiction surfacing discipline — disagreement is **always** preserved in registry, never resolved in conversation alone.
- Reviewer-by-reviewer audit trail (R5) — even unanimous decisions record individual verdicts.
- "Productive disagreement" celebrated explicitly; conflict avoidance recognized as failure mode.
- Periodic devil's advocate assignment — one steward per review explicitly tasked with opposition.

**Recovery:** Past N cycles audited for surface consensus; underlying disagreements surfaced retrospectively; affected positions reopened for genuine contestation.

---

## 14. Compound stewardship failures

★ Hypothesis — common compound patterns:

- **SF1 + SF2** — Stewardship capture + ideology hardening. Captured steward defends positions ideologically; other stewards defer; corpus drifts toward captured steward's personal commitments.
- **SF4 + SF9** — Authority centralization + form-over-substance. Centralized authority performs compliance form; substance evaluation has no independent reviewer.
- **SF5 + SF6** — Drift normalization + maintenance fatigue. Tired stewards normalize what they no longer have energy to address.
- **SF7 + SF8** — Fossilization + complacency. "Settled" positions plus "mature stewardship" framing produces immobile, self-satisfied corpus.
- **SF10 + SF2** — Conflict avoidance + ideology hardening. Disagreement is suppressed; ideologically-held positions go unchallenged.

★ Hypothesis: compound failures degrade faster than the sum of individual failures. Stewards should track compound risks explicitly.

## 15. Self-recognition discipline

T5 only works if stewards can **recognize these failures in themselves**. The hardest part of stewardship is honest self-observation.

★ Hypothesis practices for self-recognition:

- **Annual personal-failure inventory.** Each steward writes a private note: "which of SF1-SF10 do I see in myself?" The note is private; the practice is shared.
- **Peer-feedback exchange.** Stewards exchange brief notes on which failure modes they observe in each other. Conducted with care; framed as practice support, not critique.
- **External voice.** Periodically, non-stewards (researchers, readers, external practitioners) are asked to observe stewardship and name what they see.
- **Departure debrief.** Outgoing stewards write candid notes about failure modes they observed but did not fully address.

★ Hypothesis: self-recognition is **uncomfortable**. The discipline is to practice discomfort, not to eliminate it.

## 16. Anti-patterns (T5-specific)

- **Failure-mode dismissal.** "These don't apply to us."
- **Moralization.** Failure modes framed as character flaws rather than practice patterns.
- **External attribution.** Failure modes attributed only to other stewardships, never to this one.
- **Failure-mode adoption performative.** Catalog cited in audits but not used in self-reflection.
- **Naming-without-action.** Stewards identify failures in themselves but do not change practice.
- **Failure-mode ideology.** Some stewards become obsessed with one failure mode and apply it inappropriately to other situations.
- **Public shaming.** Failure modes used as critique weapons rather than reflective tools.
- **Crisis-only invocation.** Catalog consulted only after visible failure; ignored during ongoing practice.
- **New-steward exemption.** Catalog applied to senior stewards only; new stewards assumed exempt from SF6, SF7.
- **Forgetting.** Catalog created but not revisited; failure modes drift back unnoticed.

## 17. Stewardship summary (mandatory per runner)

### Stewardship reasoning
T5 frames stewardship as **fallible practice** that requires **explicit failure naming** to remain healthy. The 10 failure modes (SF1-SF10) describe specific degradation patterns observable in human practice over time. The steward's posture is: failure modes are **present in every stewardship**; the discipline is to make them visible enough to address.

### Failure / survivability implication
Stewardship failures **precede** corpus failures by years. R10 corpus failures are often the visible expression of T5 stewardship failures that began earlier and went unaddressed. T5 is the **early warning** layer of long-term corpus survivability.

### Corpus continuity implication
A corpus's continuity depends on stewardship continuity, and stewardship continuity depends on **honest failure recognition**. Stewardships that cannot name their failures will exhibit them; corpora stewarded by such stewardships degrade.

### Institutional memory implication
The failure mode catalog is itself institutional memory — a record of patterns observed across stewardships that informs future stewards. T5 is appended over decades as new patterns emerge.

### Drift / entropy implication
Many T5 failures (SF3 hidden simplification, SF5 drift normalization, SF6 maintenance fatigue, SF7 fossilization) are **slow-entropy** patterns that accumulate over years. T5 is the discipline that surfaces them while remediation remains feasible.

### Revision governance proposal
- T5 catalog itself is subject to R5 governance — additions reflect observed patterns.
- Self-recognition exercise outputs feed into stewardship rotation decisions.
- Compound failure detection routes to charter review (R5 C8).
- T5 catalog reviewed annually as part of stewardship cadence.

## 19. Closing invariant

> A stewardship that believes it does not fail will fail in all ten ways.
> A stewardship that names its failures honestly may still fail — but slower, more visibly, and more recoverably.
> The catalog is not for accusation. It is for practice.

★ Hypothesis: in 20 years, the most valuable T5 artifact may be the accumulated **departure debriefs** — stewards leaving the system who wrote candidly about what they saw. Those notes capture the practice's degradation patterns better than any synchronic catalog can.

T5 declares the patterns. The practice is to recognize them — first in others, then, with more discipline, in oneself.
