# T0 — Theory Stewardship Charter

> Generalized — institutional WaaS architecture corpus, theory stewardship layer.
> All generalized reasoning is ★ Hypothesis.
> No final theory framing. No permanent correctness claim. No hidden revisionism. No authority absolutism.

## 0. Position in the corpus

The corpus reached **publication state** at Stage 32 (44 docs: 33 D + 6 C + 5 E) and was **operationally bounded** at Stage 33 (55 docs: + 11 R = R-series Reasoning Operations Layer).

R-series defines **discipline specifications** — what retrieval must do, what reasoning must produce, what contradictions must be preserved, what AI must not do, what failure modes the corpus must guard against.

Stage 34 adds the **T-series Theory Stewardship Layer** — the **practice manual** for living stewards operating the corpus under R-series discipline. Where R-series says "discipline X is required," T-series says "this is what discipline X looks like when a steward does it on a Tuesday afternoon."

## 1. Core thesis

> A mature institutional reasoning system survives not by remaining unchanged, but by evolving slowly, visibly, and reconstructably under disciplined stewardship.
> Long-lived institutional theories are governed ecosystems of evolving constraints, not frozen collections of truths.
> The discipline lives in the practice, not in the specification.

---

## 2. ≠ propositions (charter level)

- Discipline specification ≠ stewardship practice
- R-series rule ≠ steward's weekly task
- Corpus completeness ≠ corpus survivability
- Frozen invariants ≠ surviving invariants
- Visible evolution ≠ permitted instability
- Steward continuity ≠ knowledge continuity
- Authority ≠ correctness
- Stewardship ≠ ownership
- Maintenance ≠ improvement
- Practice ≠ procedure

## 3. Why a stewardship layer is needed

R-series alone is **insufficient**. R-series specifies disciplines, but disciplines without a practice tradition tend to:

- **Degrade over time** — rules become rote, then ignored, then forgotten.
- **Concentrate in original authors** — only the discipline's designer truly knows what compliance means.
- **Become brittle under stewardship transition** — incoming stewards inherit rules without the practice that makes them load-bearing.
- **Drift into convenience** — exceptions accumulate; original intent is lost.
- **Mask their own failure** — stewards continue to perform the form of compliance after the substance has decayed.

T-series exists to **embed R-series into practice**. The practice tradition is what makes the discipline survive personnel rotation, tooling change, and institutional pressure.

★ Hypothesis: a corpus with strong R-series specification and weak T-series practice will degrade within one to two stewardship rotations (3-5 years). A corpus with strong T-series practice can survive weaker R-series specifications by reconstructing rules from accumulated craft knowledge.

## 5. Stewardship spirit (10 commitments)

Stage 32 closed with 10 corpus-spirit commitments. Stage 34 declares 10 **stewardship spirit** commitments — what stewards practice, not what the corpus claims.

1. **Visible evolution.** Every change leaves a trace a future steward can read.
2. **Recoverable reasoning.** A new steward six years from now can reconstruct why a current invariant exists.
3. **Explicit uncertainty.** The steward writes "we don't know" before writing "we believe."
4. **Human accountability.** No decision of record is anonymized or delegated to tooling.
5. **Conservative adaptation.** When in doubt, the steward defers change and surfaces the question.
6. **Survivability over elegance.** The steward chooses durable framing over clever framing.
7. **Append-only history.** The steward never edits what was previously published — only appends, supersedes, or annotates.
8. **Anti-hype discipline.** The steward refuses industry buzzwords until they have survived E4 institutionalization.
9. **Cross-domain consistency.** The steward checks cluster interactions before approving cluster-local changes.
10. **Contradiction surfacing.** The steward names contradictions explicitly rather than smoothing them.

These commitments are **practiced**, not just declared. T1-T5 specify what practicing them looks like.

## 6. Stewardship invariants (always present)

★ Hypothesis — minimum stewardship invariants that survive all rotations:

| Invariant | Operational meaning |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Evidence-first reasoning** | Stewards cite the corpus or external evidence; they do not assert from memory or intuition without naming the source |
| **Explicit uncertainty retention** | ★ markers survive every steward operation |
| **Survivability over elegance** | When two framings exist, the more conservative one is preferred even if it is uglier |
| **Append-only conceptual history** | No edit-in-place of published content; supersede or annotate |
| **Human accountability preservation** | Every decision of record carries a named human |
| **Visible revision history** | Audit trail (R5) is treated as a primary artifact, not overhead |
| **Anti-hype discipline** | New industry concepts pass through E4 before entering corpus |
| **Cross-domain consistency** | Cluster changes reviewed for inter-cluster implications |
| **Explicit contradiction surfacing** | T3/T5 contradictions are surfaced in synthesis, not smoothed |
| **Reconstructable reasoning continuity** | A six-year-removed reader can rebuild the reasoning trace |

These are not ten separate disciplines — they are **one stewardship posture** with ten observable manifestations.

## 7. Stewardship anti-patterns (always avoided)

★ Hypothesis — practices that signal stewardship degradation:

| Anti-pattern | What it looks like in practice |
|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Silent rewriting** | Steward edits an old doc to "fix" outdated framing without snapshot |
| **Retrospective certainty fabrication** | Steward removes ★ markers from prior content because "we know now" |
| **Historical invariant erasure** | Steward deletes superseded invariants instead of preserving them |
| **Governance-by-convenience** | Cooling-off periods waived "just this once" |
| **AI-generated conceptual drift** | Steward accepts AI-drafted content that introduces subtle terminology shifts |
| **Survivorship bias** | Steward only references the disciplines that worked, not the ones that failed |
| **Ideology hardening** | Steward defends a position because they wrote it, not because evidence supports it |
| **Speculative contamination** | Frontier content cited in operational contexts without ★ |
| **Complexity inflation** | Steward adds nuance until the doc is unreadable to fresh readers |
| **False coherence pressure** | Steward smooths contradictions to make the corpus "feel finished" |

T5 (Stewardship Failure Modes) catalogues these as named failure modes; T0 declares them as **always-avoided** practice patterns.

## 8. Stewardship cadence (operational rhythm)

★ Hypothesis — minimum viable cadence for a corpus of this scale:

| Cadence | Activity |
|---------|----------|
| **Weekly** | Reader-report triage; AI-output sampling; trace audit (R9) |
| **Monthly** | C0-C2 change processing (R5); contradiction registry review |
| **Quarterly** | C3-C4 change cycle; cluster lead sync; cross-cluster consistency check |
| **Semi-annual** | C5 new-doc review; reader-feedback aggregation; drift signal review |
| **Annual** | C6-C7 changes; ontology audit (R4); decay-class re-assessment (R6); zone boundary review (R8) |
| **Multi-year** | C8 charter review; stewardship rotation; major restructure proposals |

The cadence is **explicit**. A steward who cannot describe their own weekly / monthly / quarterly rhythm is not yet practicing stewardship — they are reacting to events.

## 9. Stewardship artifacts (what stewards produce)

Stewards produce six classes of artifact, in addition to corpus content:

1. **Audit trail entries** (R5) — every change recorded with rationale.
2. **Snapshot triggers** (R7) — every supersession captured with worldview annotation.
3. **Contradiction registry entries** (R3) — every surfaced tension classified.
4. **Ontology registry updates** (R4) — every sense addition / version documented.
5. **Stewardship handoff documents** (R5 §11) — every rotation accompanied by knowledge transfer.
6. **Quarterly drift reports** (T1) — observable corpus signals summarized.

These artifacts are the **operational substance** of stewardship. A steward whose only output is corpus content is performing **authorship**, not **stewardship**.

---

## 10. Stewardship is not authorship

A common conflation: stewardship is treated as a senior form of authorship.

It is not. Authorship produces content; stewardship produces **conditions under which content remains usable**.

- An author asks: "Is this doc correct?"
- A steward asks: "Will this doc still be interpretable in 2036?"

- An author asks: "Does this contradict prior content?"
- A steward asks: "How is the contradiction surfaced to readers? How is it registered? Who is responsible for revisiting it?"

- An author asks: "Is this hypothesis well-formed?"
- A steward asks: "Is the ★ marker propagating through retrieval, synthesis, and citation?"

★ Hypothesis: institutions that treat stewardship as senior authorship produce stewards who write more docs but maintain fewer. The corpus grows; survivability declines.

## 11. Stewardship transition discipline

Stewardship rotation is **the highest-risk event** for institutional knowledge. Most R-series disciplines survive rotation if the rotation is **disciplined**; most fail if it is **abrupt**.

Transition discipline (★ Hypothesis):

1. **Overlap window** — incoming and outgoing stewards co-steward for ≥ 3 months.
2. **Handoff document** — outgoing steward writes a doc covering: in-flight contradictions, pending proposals, open ontology questions, AI-assistance configuration history, reader-report patterns, current drift signals.
3. **Trail walk** — incoming steward reads the last N audit trail entries (★ N = 100) with outgoing steward present for context.
4. **Snapshot exercise** — incoming steward retrieves a random R7 snapshot from N years ago and reconstructs its worldview. If they cannot, R7 is in failure mode (FM7).
5. **Reader-report cadence reset** — incoming steward establishes their own response cadence; previous SLAs are reference, not obligation.
6. **First-quarter no-major-change** — incoming steward avoids C3+ changes for the first quarter; the focus is observation, not revision.

★ Hypothesis: rotation done well is **slow** — and looks expensive. Rotation done quickly is fast and looks efficient — and is the single most common cause of stewardship vacuum (R10 FM3) two to three years later.

## 12. T-series and R-series — the read-as-a-pair model

R-series and T-series are designed to be **read together**:

- A new steward reads R-series for **what the rules are**.
- A new steward reads T-series for **what compliance looks like in practice**.
- An auditor reviews R-series for **what was supposed to happen**.
- An auditor reviews T-series for **what stewards actually did**.

★ Hypothesis: institutions that adopt only R-series produce **bureaucratic** stewardship — rule-following without judgment. Institutions that adopt only T-series produce **artisanal** stewardship — craft without discipline. The pair is load-bearing; either alone is insufficient.

## 13. What T-series is NOT

★ Hypothesis (charter clarifications):

- T-series is **not a project management methodology**. It does not specify task tracking tools, sprint cadences, or roadmap formats.
- T-series is **not a hiring guide**. Steward selection is institutional; T-series specifies what stewards do, not who becomes one.
- T-series is **not an AI agent prompt**. R9 constrains AI; T-series constrains human practice.
- T-series is **not a checklist**. T1-T5 are practice patterns, not closed enumerations.
- T-series is **not permanent**. It evolves under R5 governance like every other corpus layer.

## 15. The "honest practice" stance

T-series adopts a posture of **honest practice**. Stewards periodically ask, in writing:

> "If a new steward joined tomorrow, would they be able to do what I am currently doing — and would they want to?"

If the answer to either half is "no," something in the practice has become un-transferable or unsustainable. T-series exists to make practice transferable and sustainable.

A stewardship that **only the current steward can perform** is functionally a single point of failure (R10 FM3 incoming). A stewardship that **the current steward dreads** is heading toward fatigue collapse (T5).

---

## 16. Closing invariant

> Discipline lives in practice, not in specification.
> A practice tradition that survives stewardship rotation is the difference between a corpus that endures and a corpus that fossilizes.
> T-series is not a more polished R-series. It is the layer that makes R-series load-bearing in human hands.

★ Hypothesis: in 20 years, the most important measure of this corpus's success will not be its content but its **practice tradition** — the observable habits, artifacts, and cadences of the stewards who maintained it. If the practice survived, the content survived. If the practice did not survive, the content is a museum exhibit regardless of how good it once was.

T0 declares the practice. T1-T5 specify it.
